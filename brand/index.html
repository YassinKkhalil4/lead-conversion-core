The idea

A K, and the reply leaving it.

The mark is the letter K built from three strokes: a stem, and two arms. The upper arm is ink and points back into the letter. The lower arm is green and travels out of it, past where the letterform would stop. The K holds; the reply leaves.

This is why the green belongs to the lower arm and only to it. Colour both arms and the K flattens into a monogram. Colour the upper one instead and the movement runs backwards, into the mark rather than out of it.

The lockup

Fixed proportions, two finishes.

The lockup is a single locked artwork at 256 × 64. The K is drawn between x 12.5 and x 51.5, caps included, and the wordmark starts at x 74 — so 22.5 units of air sit between the arm tips and the k. Scale it by height and let the width follow. Never rebuild it by setting the wordmark next to the mark yourself; that gap is already resolved.

Primary · on paper

Reversed · on brand ink

The green arm is the only part that holds its colour on every ground. The stem and upper arm invert with the background, which is the mark's one fragility: on a mid-tone — a photograph, a mid-grey, a saturated fill — neither the ink nor the reversed version has enough separation and the K stops reading as a letter. Place the lockup on paper, on brand ink, or on the pale surface tint. Nowhere else.

Marks and icons

The strokes thicken when it gets small.

The K is one letterform, so it survives scaling down further than a compound mark would. What does not survive is the stroke: inside the icon tile the glyph is set at width 7 and scaled to 78%, which at 16px leaves barely more than a pixel. The small icon keeps the same three strokes at width 9 and drops the inset, so the letter stays a letter. Nothing else about it changes.

Clear space

Half the lockup's height, on every side.

One rule, easy to check: measure the lockup's height, halve it, and keep that much empty on all four sides. A lockup set 26px tall wants 13px of clear space. The K's arms reach further right than the stem does, so measure from the artwork's box and not from the strokes. Nothing sits inside it — no rules, no type, no photo edge, no button.

Dashed line marks the outer boundary. The tinted block is the lockup's own 256 × 64 box.

Misuse

What breaks it.

Do

Scale by height

Set a height, let width follow. The lockup's ratio is fixed at 256 × 64.

Don't

Stretch or squash

Any non-uniform scale changes the angle of the two arms, and the symmetry between them is what makes the letter read as a K.

Do

Keep the green on the lower arm

Stem and upper arm in ink, lower arm in green. That is the reply leaving the letter.

Don't

Move the green, or colour both arms

On the upper arm the movement runs backwards. On both, the K flattens into a monogram.

Do

Use the reversed art on dark

There is a made-for-purpose file. Only the stem and upper arm invert in it — the green arm is unchanged.

Don't

Place it over photos or mid-tones

Neither the ink nor the reversed art separates from a mid-tone, and the K stops reading. Paper, brand ink, or surface tint only.

Do

Leave the green alone

The lower arm is always #00B368, on every ground.

Don't

Recolour, rotate, or add effects

No gradients, shadows, outlines, or tilts. It points forward because a reply moves forward.

Colour

One ink, two greens, and a reason for the second.

The brand green is bright by design — it is the moment the reply lands. But at #00B368 it reaches only 2.75:1 against white, which fails WCAG AA for text. So the green splits by surface: the brand value on dark grounds, a darkened version of the same hue for anything that has to be read on light.

Brand ink

#0C1F1A

Body type on light, and the ground for every dark surface. A green-biased near-black, never pure #000.

Brand green

#00B368

The mark's lower arm, always. As a UI accent it is for dark grounds only — 6.24:1 on brand ink.

Deep green

#007A47

Accent text, links and filled buttons on light grounds. 5.42:1 on white, 5.04:1 on surface tint.

Surface tint

#F5F7F6

Alternating section grounds. Carries the same green bias as the ink, so it never reads as cold grey.

Measured contrast. Every pair in use clears WCAG AA for normal text.

Typography

The system stack, on purpose.

No webfont. A brokerage opens the page on a mid-range Android over a hotel connection, and a font that arrives late is a page that flashes. The system stack renders instantly and looks native on every device — San Francisco on Apple, Segoe on Windows, Roboto on Android. The monospace face is the second voice, reserved for anything measured: figures, phone numbers, scores, labels, timings.

The cost of slow response

The wordmark is live text, not outlined paths. It asks for Helvetica Neue and falls back to Helvetica or Arial, so its measured width varies slightly by machine. The lockup's 256 × 64 box carries enough room to the right of the word to absorb that, but the fallback is still a real difference between one render and the next.

This is a working compromise, not the finished state. When the identity is next touched, convert the wordmark to outlines — then the file is truly self-contained and the fallback question disappears.

Voice

Arithmetic, not adjectives.

The product's strongest argument is a number and its strongest proof is a live phone number. The writing should get out of the way of both. Sales-oriented is fine. Salesy is not.

  • Lead with what it costs them today

    Open on the reader's problem, not the software's features. The spend is already gone; the question is what happens in the next five minutes.

    “You have already paid for the lead.” — not “Powerful AI-driven lead qualification.”

  • No exclamation marks, no hype vocabulary

    Nothing is revolutionary, game-changing, seamless, or effortless. No emoji anywhere — not in copy, not as section markers.

  • Name the limit before someone else does

    Where a claim has a caveat, the caveat ships with it. Research gets its funding disclosed; mockups are labelled as sample data.

    “Five of ten weighted factors shown.”

  • Never imply customers we do not have

    No testimonials, logos, case studies, or “trusted by”. Positioning by market is fine — Dubai first, Egypt second — but a market is not a customer.

  • Be precise about what ships

    Arabic and English ship and are verified. Other languages are written and loaded during setup. Never name a language we have not built, and never imply a menu.

  • The brand stays neutral international

    The product runs in Arabic; the identity does not signal it. No Arabic script in the logo, no geometric or regional visual motifs. There is deliberately no Arabic-script lockup — the mark is the mark in every market.
